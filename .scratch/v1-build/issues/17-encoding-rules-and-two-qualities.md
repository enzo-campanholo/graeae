# 17: Encoding rules and two qualities

**What to build:** A Chromium screen share goes out in hardware H.264 at 1080p60 and at 720p60, so a viewer on a weak link drops to 720p alone. Each viewer can choose "Auto", "1080p" or "720p" per watched screen share, and a share received at the lower quality carries a "720p" mark. Firefox sends one quality in VP9.

The rules are fixed numbers with no settings. Sources for what works: `prototype/quality-proof` and `docs/research/capture-rate-and-hardware-encode.md` on the branch `research/capture-rate`. Neither branch is merged; read them with `git show <branch>:<path>`.

Spec: [Encoding and quality](../spec.md#encoding-and-quality).

UI: build with the impeccable skill, following `DESIGN.md`.

**Blocked by:** 15

**Status:** resolved

- [x] Codec order: H.264 baseline profile (`42001f`), then one software codec last so a share always starts; never constrained baseline, main or high, never HEVC (changed from "high, then main" by the owner's check below; the browser offers the first it has, alone)
- [x] Capture is asked for 120 fps and the sender is limited to 60
- [x] Firefox screen share: one quality, VP9 at 1080p, limited to 30 fps and 6 Mbps
- [x] Webcam share follows the same codec rule as a Chromium screen share
- [x] One codec per share, with no per-viewer codec choice
- [x] The media layer seam gains "change a viewer's quality", in the fake and in the Cloudflare implementation

- [x] The fake media client can report which quality a watched share is being received at, so the "720p" mark under Auto is testable

- [x] A share received at the lower quality carries a small "720p" mark
- [x] A single-quality share (Firefox screen, webcam) shows no quality choice
- [x] Two qualities can be switched off in one place, leaving one 1080p60 quality at 12 Mbps with no choice and no mark (switched off: the media server freezes the 720p quality of an H.264 share; ticket 29)
- [x] The backend records each share's maximum bitrate (12, 6 or 1.5 Mbps) for the usage count

- **The pure rule** is `encodingPlan(browser, kind)` in `src/shared/encoding.ts`, tested row by row against the spec's table in `test/encoding.test.ts`. It gives the codec order, the qualities (name, rid, height, frame-rate and bitrate limits), what the capture is asked for, and the maximum bitrate. Chromium screen: H.264 high, main, then VP8; 1080p60 at 12 Mbps (rid `f`) and 720p60 at 4 Mbps (rid `h`); capture asked for up to 1920x1080 at 120 fps. Firefox screen: VP9, then VP8; one 1080p30 quality at 6 Mbps; capture asked for 30 fps. Webcam, any browser: the Chromium codec rule; one 720p30 quality at 1.5 Mbps. **The switch** is `TWO_QUALITIES` in the same file: false leaves a Chromium screen share one 1080p60 quality at 12 Mbps, and since a share is then listed with one quality, the choice and the mark go with it.
- **Decisions.** VP8 is the software codec: every browser encodes and decodes it, so a share always starts. H.264 is offered only in packetization mode 1, never constrained baseline (`42e0`), plain baseline (`4200`) or HEVC. A larger capture is scaled down to each quality's height (`scaleResolutionDownBy`), never up, besides the capture's own 1080p cap. A Firefox capture is asked for 30 fps, its limit, rather than 120.
- **The browser's side** is `src/app/lib/media/encodings.ts` (pure, tested): `codecPreferences` filters `RTCRtpSender.getCapabilities` to the plan's codecs in order, with rtx, red and ulpfec after; `sendEncodings` makes the encodings; `receivedQuality` reads a frame height as 720p (720 lines or fewer) or 1080p. `browser.ts` adds the transceiver with the encodings, sets the codec preferences (a browser that refuses them keeps its own order), sets the limits again after the answer, and reads `inbound-rtp` `frameHeight` every 2 seconds for each watched share. No `contentHint` or `degradationPreference` is set: that is **ticket 18**'s.
- **Protocol.** `shares.start` for a screen share carries `browser` ("chromium" or "firefox"); the instance runs the same rule on it, and refuses a screen share without one. A webcam's plan is the same from every browser. `ListedShare` gains `twoQualities: true` on a share with two. The watching area gains the request `{ type: "quality", share, quality }` ("auto", "1080p" or "720p"), the change `quality` (to that tab only), and `WatchingState.qualities`, the tab's watched shares at anything but Auto. The instance always answers a choice for a share the tab watches with the quality now in effect: the one chosen, or the one it had when a check or the media server failed; a share with one quality is always "auto". A choice for a share the tab does not watch, or naming no quality graeae has, gets nothing.
- **Records.** Each share gains `qualities` (1 or 2) and `max_bitrate` in bits per second (12, 6 or 1.5 Mbps), each watch `quality`. Shares recorded before the migration count as one quality, 12 Mbps for a screen and 1.5 for a webcam. A watch starts at Auto, keeps its choice through a reconnect and a restart, and a new watch of the same share starts at Auto again.
- **The media layer** gains `setQuality(connection, from, track, quality)`, and `pull` takes an optional `preferred` for the track sent at two qualities. On Cloudflare that is simulcast: a pull's remote track carries `simulcast`, and a change is `PUT sessions/{id}/tracks/update` with the pulled track's `mid`, `sessionId`, `trackName` and `simulcast`. Auto is `preferredRid: "f"` with `priorityOrdering: "asciibetical"`, so the media server steps this viewer alone down when their link is weak; "1080p" and "720p" are `f` or `h` with `priorityOrdering: "none"`. All three have `ridNotAvailable: "asciibetical"`, so when the sharer stops sending 1080p (**ticket 18**'s weak upload) every viewer falls to 720p. The fake records each pulled two-quality track's preference (`fakeMediaControl.preferred()`) and can fail `setQuality`.
- **The page.** A watched two-quality share leads its pill of controls with "Auto", "1080p" and "720p", a segmented control like the one on "You" (radio buttons, one tab stop, arrow keys move the choice). The page shows a choice as made at once and takes the instance's answer as final. While such a share arrives at 720p its caption ends with a small "720p" tag ("Received at 720p" to a screen reader). In the strip under a focused share the choice is left out (the pill is too small), and the mark stays. The fake media client reports the received quality from `graeaeFakeMedia.received` and `receive(quality)` (1080p by default).
- **Tests.** `test/encoding.test.ts` (27): the plan for every row, the switch, the codec order on Chromium's and Firefox's capability lists, the encodings and the scale, the received quality. `test/quality.test.ts` (Seam 1, 11): listed with two qualities and pulled at Auto; one viewer at 720p while another keeps Auto; to 1080p and back; nobody else told; the choice through a restart; Auto again on a new watch; a failed media server; not watched; not a quality; a Firefox screen share and a webcam with one quality. `test/migrations.test.ts` gains the two-qualities migration on an instance with members and rooms. `test/capture.test.ts`: the capture asked for 120 fps on Chromium and 30 on Firefox. Seam 2, `test/browser/quality.spec.ts` (members Kai and Lou): the choice on the screen and none on the webcam, the mark under Auto and on the screen alone, 1080p by click, 720p by the arrow keys, Auto again when watched again; a Firefox screen share with no choice and no mark. - **Cloudflare refused high and main.** Offered H.264 high, main, then VP8 on a share sent at two qualities, the media server answered VP8, encoded in software (`libvpx`). Offered every H.264 profile, it chose constrained baseline `42e01f`: OpenH264, software. Offered baseline `42001f` alone, it took it, and both encodings ran on `MediaFoundationVideoEncodeAccelerator (NVIDIA H.264 Encoder MFT)`, `powerEfficientEncoder: true`. `encodingInfo` agreed: `42001f` and `640032` power efficient; VP9 and AV1 not. Quality proof had high profile accepted because it sent one quality.
- **Cloudflare ignores the offer's order.** Offered baseline then VP8, it answered VP8. So `codecPreferences` (`src/app/lib/media/encodings.ts`) now gives the browser the first of the plan's codecs it has, alone: H.264 baseline, or VP8 on a browser without it; VP9 alone for a Firefox screen share, which the owner checked starts and plays. H.264's match now reads four hex digits, so `42e0` is never taken for `42`.
- **Firefox viewers** decode the baseline share (question 3 below is settled): the media server describes it to viewers as `42e01f`, as it did high profile, and Chromium decodes it in hardware (`D3D11VideoDecoder`).
- **Two encodes on hardware:** `f` 1920x1080 and `h` 1280x720, each about 56 to 60 fps at 120 Hz (47 at 144 Hz, the capture limit), under 12 and 4 Mbps, `qualityLimitationReason` none. CS2's frame rate held with the share on.
- **The 720p quality freezes on Cloudflare.** A viewer at 720p (or at Auto once stepped down) froze for about half the time: 24 freezes and 58 seconds frozen in 2 minutes, each ending with a key frame. The sharer sent `h` without a break (about 570 frames every 10 seconds); the viewer kept receiving packets, often in bursts of small ones, but built no frames from them, with no packet lost, until its key frame request reached the encoder. In Chromium and Firefox alike, at Auto and at a fixed 720p, with `ridNotAvailable` "asciibetical" or "none". The same 720p in VP8 played without a freeze for 2.5 minutes, and 1080p in H.264 never froze. The likeliest reading is the media server's padding while it probes for bandwidth to move the viewer up, which H.264 receivers lose frames to and VP8 ones do not; Cloudflare documents nothing of it. DevTools' network throttling does not reach WebRTC media, so Auto's step-down on a weak link was never really tested.
