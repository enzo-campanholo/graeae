# 62: A hand-over, or a lost instance, during a replacement of the receiving connection

**Status:** resolved

**Blocked by:** none

**Why:** ticket 61's review found edge cases in replacing a receiving connection. A replacement lasts a few seconds, happens at an 11-hour renewal on the relay or after the browser gives the connection up, and nothing here has been seen on `graeae-test`. Each case below needs a decision before it is built.

Triage (2026-11-23) kept the first two findings here, since both are in the replacement's own code. The other two have their own tickets: the usage overlap is 63, and asking again after `unavailable` is 64. The agent brief under Comments is the contract.

## What was found

- **A hand-over during a replacement can apply an offer out of order.** While a tab replaces its receiving connection, a hand-over moves its watch of the old share onto the replacement at once (`Shares.move`, `receivingTarget`), and the tab hears `moved`. The room client runs `moved` behind the replacement on the receiving connection's turn. So it applies that offer after the replacement's later `pulled` offers. And a move that began on the old connection, before the replacement existed, can finish after it does: its offer is for the old session, but the tab applies it to the new connection. Either way the moved picture can be lost. One way out is for the instance to treat a move during a replacement as one of the replacement's steps, sent in turn as `pulled`. Whatever the fix, Seam 1 and a journey should cover it.
- **A replacement waits in memory.** `Instance.replacing` and `Shares.pending` live only in memory. If the Durable Object is evicted between a `pulled` and the tab's `answer` while the socket stays open, the next step never comes. The tab's wait for the next `pulled` or `replaced` has no timeout, so every later watch on that tab queues behind it. After an instance restart, a `role = 'replacement'` row is cleared only when the tab asks again (`replaceAgain`, on its next socket). Check whether a watch's own pending pull has the same gap.
- **Usage misses the overlap.** The tab reads the old connection's final total just before `replace`, but the old connection keeps receiving until the old pulls are closed and it is retired. Those bytes, a second or two per replacement, are never counted. The spec has the count never read below Cloudflare's own figure.
- **No limit on asking again.** If a replacement is answered `unavailable` while the browser still says the receiving connection `failed`, `recover("receiving")` asks again every 2 seconds for as long as the tab is open. On the sending side the 30-second rule ends this. On the receiving side nothing does, so the `unavailable` cases need telling apart (the media server failed, versus nothing to replace).

## Comments

Built on `v1-build`, with typecheck, lint and `pnpm test` green (508 Vitest tests, 69 Playwright journeys and loopback cases). The Seam 1 cases are in `test/replacing.test.ts`.

- **A move during a replacement is one of its steps.** Once the replacement has a media session, a move pulls nothing. It puts the new share on the watcher's stage, on the current connection with no pull, and the replacement pulls it as one of its `pulled` steps. A move whose pull returns to find the tab's receiving target changed closes that pull and is done again on the current target. If the new share ended, or the watcher left, while the old pull closed, the watcher hears `unwatched` at once. A quality the move changes now goes out by itself (`requalified`), not with `moved`.
- **The tab drops a `moved` it heard before a replacement went through.** The instance can send `moved` for the old connection just before it handles the tab's `replace`. The tab then runs that `moved` behind the replacement, which has already pulled the new share onto the new connection as a step. So the room client counts replacements once `connected` arrives, and leaves alone a `moved` heard before the count changed. The journey (`handover.spec.ts`, Elio) holds the tab's `replace` until the `moved` has arrived. It fails without this guard, the tab sending one answer too many.
- **Progress comes from storage.** `Instance.replacing` and `Shares.pending` are gone. An answer from a tab with a replacement under way (`media_connection.role = 'replacement'` with a media session) starts its next step. A watch whose pull onto the replacement has returned, and which is not yet watching, counts as carried for `finishReplacement`, `end()` and the sweep, and a stage entry with no pull counts as under way for the sweep while a replacement is. Only `Instance.stepping` is held in memory: it marks a step under way, and the making of the replacement itself, so an answer meanwhile starts no second step.
- **Eviction.** The harness gains `evict()` (`evictDurableObject` from the Workers test pool), which keeps the sockets hibernated. `test/harness.test.ts` covers it. The criterion is automated: an eviction between a `pulled` and its answer, then a lost tab that makes the wake-up sweep every connection, then the answer. The sweep closes nothing, and the replacement goes on to `replaced`.
- **Leaving mid-replacement.** Leaving cleanly or by grace, at a `pulled`, during a held pull, or during a held `sessions/new`, leaves no replacement connection on record and no pull on it. The last of these was a real leak: a replacement whose session came back after its tab had gone was never forgotten. `replaceNext` now forgets it.
- **A failed second replacement.** If a replacement is let go of for a new one (the tab asked again) and the new one cannot be made, the shares a hand-over put on the stage for the first to pull leave the stage with `unwatched`.
- **Loopback: not added.** Its stand-in has one peer for each purpose and no replacement, and it has no instance to hand over through. A hand-over during a replacement would need a second receiving peer and the instance's step order played in the page.
- **Left for 64.** A replacement cut short by a lost socket, with the next one answered `unavailable`: the tab's media is already on the new peer, which the instance never took as current, whatever happens to a `moved`. `docs/media-server.md` notes as not yet seen what a close does to a pull whose offer was never answered.
- **From the review, not done.** The finding that a share pulled twice within one replacement leaves the first transceiver behind does not apply: `drop` in `browser.ts` does nothing, and the media server's next offer settles the transceivers.

**Checked on `graeae-test` (2026-11-24): a hand-over that ends the share a replacement is pulling stalls the watcher for good.** `063611c` deployed (version `f3f2f775`). From this server's virtual desktop, in "First room": Linux R1 shared a 1280x720 game window, and Linux R2 watched it. R1 opened a second tab (`newtab`, `share screen`), then pressed "Share from here" (`handover screen`). 0.3 s later, R2 ran `fail receiving`. R2's picture froze, and it never came back: 1399 frames decoded, then nothing for over 4 minutes. A second try at 14:49:10 UTC froze the same way at 209 frames, so 2 of 2. Unwatching and watching again did nothing. Only a reload brought R2 back.

From the instance's lines (`.logs/graeae-test-1795531200000-1795531680000.jsonl` and `.logs/graeae-test-1795531200000-1795531980000.jsonl`, tab `3c4a9d18`), the first try went like this:
- 14:44:09.266: `media.replacing`. The replacement (`eff49815…`) gets its media session at 09.390, and the tab hears `connected`.
- 09.901: the hand-over ends the old share `277be8d1`.
- 09.914: the replacement's pull of that share returns, and is closed at 09.980 (`watch.ended`, `wasWatching: false`).
- 10.795: `handover.moved`, with no `step: true`. The new share is pulled onto the old receiving connection `f97ae727…` (mid 1), and the tab is sent `moved` with that offer.
- After that, the instance sends no `pulled`, `replaced` or `unavailable`. The tab answers nothing, and reports `received` 0 bytes every minute.
- In R2's page, the replacement `RTCPeerConnection` stays `new`, with no remote description. The old one keeps only mid 0.

The cause, in `Shares.replaceNext` (`src/instance/shares.ts`):
- When a pull onto the replacement returns and the share has gone, the step closes that pull and calls `forgetIfEmpty(replacementId)`, even though the tab is still open. With nothing else on it, the replacement's row is deleted.
- The loop's next pass finds no replacement and returns `"stale"`. `Instance.replaceStep` sends nothing for `"stale"`.
- So `receivingTarget` falls back to the old connection, and the move pulls onto it. The tab never hears that its replacement is over. Its receiving turn waits for a `pulled` or `replaced` that never comes, and later watches queue behind it.

The fakes cannot show this, since there the move's timing never lands between the replacement's pull and its return. Not fixed here; filed and fixed as [67](67-a-hand-over-during-a-replacement-freezes-the-watcher.md), which found two more ways in (the share leaving the stage, and the media server's 406 after an offer never answered). Linux R1 and R2 were removed afterwards.

## Agent Brief

**Category:** bug
**Summary:** A hand-over during a replacement of a tab's receiving connection no longer loses the moved picture, and an instance evicted partway through a replacement no longer stalls the tab's watching.

**Current behavior:**
Triage checked each of these against the code on `v1-build` at `aa65595`. None has been reproduced by a test.
- **A move during a replacement arrives out of order.** In a hand-over within the room, the instance pulls the new share straight onto the watcher's replacement connection and sends `moved`. The room client runs `moved` behind the replacement, on the receiving connection's turn. So it applies that offer only after `replaced`, which is after the later `pulled` offers of the same media session.
- **A move can finish on the wrong connection.** A move whose pull began before the replacement had a media session can return after the replacement has taken over. Its offer is for the old session, but the tab applies it to the new connection, and the tab's answer goes to the new session. An ordinary watch already guards against this: after its pull returns, it checks the tab's receiving target again and closes the pull if that changed. A move has no such check.
- **A replacement's progress is held only in memory.** The instance remembers two things only in memory: which tab is waiting for its next replacement step, and which pulled-again watches have been carried but are not on the stage yet. The instance's sockets hibernate, so it can be evicted between a `pulled` and the tab's answer while the socket stays open. When that happens:
  - the answer starts no next step;
  - the tab waits for `pulled` or `replaced` forever, and every later negotiation on its receiving connection queues behind that wait;
  - after the wake-up, the sweep no longer treats the carried pulls as under way, and can close them.
- An ordinary watch has no such gap. It is on the stage before its offer goes out.

**Desired behavior:**
- **Each offer during a replacement is one of its steps, in order.** If a hand-over moves a watch of a tab that is replacing its receiving connection, the tab hears the new share as one of the replacement's `pulled` steps, never as a `moved` in between. If the share left the stage, the tab gets the usual `unwatched`. After `replaced`, the tab's stage shows the new share exactly once, with its picture arriving on the new connection, and neither connection holds a pull of the old share. Decided in triage: the instance makes the move a step of the replacement. The tab does not reorder offers, because it cannot tell which session an offer is for.
- **No offer for a session the tab has let go of.** A move whose pull lands on a connection that is no longer the tab's receiving target is closed, then done again on the current target, as a step if a replacement is under way. A tab only ever gets offers for its current receiving connection or its replacement.
- **Progress comes from stored records.**
  - An answer from a tab that has a replacement receiving connection on record moves that replacement on to its next step.
  - A watch pulled onto a replacement but not yet on the stage counts as under way for the sweep.
  - So after an eviction that keeps the socket open, the tab's next answer carries on with the replacement as if nothing had happened.
  - Only one step runs at a time. An answer that arrives while a step is in flight does not start another. Holding that in memory is fine, because the instance cannot be evicted while a step is waiting on the media server.
- **The tab's wait gets no timeout.** Decided in triage: once the progress is stored, nothing it waits on can be lost while the socket stays open, and a lost socket already ends the wait.
- **Leaving mid-replacement leaves nothing behind.** A tab that leaves cleanly, or runs out its grace period, mid-replacement keeps no replacement connection on record and no pull on it.

**Key interfaces:**
- The socket's media changes `moved`, `pulled` and `replaced`, and the `replace` request. `moved` stays for a hand-over when no replacement is under way.
- `Shares.move`, `replaceNext` and `finishReplacement`, and the sweep's test for a record that is under way.
- The instance's map of tabs awaiting a replacement step (`Instance.replacing`), and `Shares.pending`. Both should be read from stored records instead (`media_connection.role = 'replacement'`, `watch.watching_at`).
- A schema change for this follows the repo's migration rules (CLAUDE.md, Migrations).

After `replaced`, its stage holds the new share once. The fake media server holds one pull of the new share on the replacement and no pull of the old share.
- [x] Seam 1: a hand-over whose pull to the watcher is held by the fake media server's controls until the watcher's replacement has finished. The watcher gets no offer for its old connection, and its stage ends up holding the new share, pulled onto its current receiving connection.
- [x] Seam 1: the instance is evicted between a `pulled` and its answer, with the socket kept open. The answer starts the next step and `replaced` follows. A sweep run between the eviction and the answer closes none of the carried pulls. The harness does not have this control yet, because `restart` drops the sockets. If the Workers test pool can evict while keeping sockets, add the control to the harness with a case in `test/harness.test.ts`. If it cannot, say so here and leave this criterion for a human.
- [x] Seam 1: two answers that arrive during one step start only one pull.
- [x] Seam 1: a tab that leaves mid-replacement keeps no replacement connection on record, and the fake media server keeps no pull on it.
- [x] A browser journey on the fakes: a watcher whose receiving connection is replaced while the share it watches is handed over ends up with a moving picture of the new share.
- [x] The loopback, if its stand-in can run a hand-over during a replacement: only a real RTCPeerConnection shows what an offer applied out of order does. *It cannot: see the comment below.*
- [x] Where spec.md, `docs/media-server.md` or the file headers describe a replacement or a move, they say what now happens.
