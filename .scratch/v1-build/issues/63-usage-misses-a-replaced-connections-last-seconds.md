## Agent Brief

**Category:** bug
**Summary:** Charge what a replaced receiving connection receives between its final report and the moment its pulls are closed.

**Current behavior:**
Before asking to replace its receiving connection, the tab sends that connection's total, marked `final`. When the new connection is made, the instance resets the tab's running total (`Usage.newTotal`), so the new connection's reports count from zero. The old connection keeps receiving each watched picture until the replacement finishes, the instance closes the old pulls, and the tab retires the old connection. Nobody reports those bytes. The instance's rule for watches with no later report (charge each watched share at its maximum bitrate) does not apply here either, because the new connection's next report covers the tab and so cancels it. On the relay, at an 11-hour renewal, counting relayed watching twice covers the gap in practice. After a failure on a direct path, nothing covers it.

**Desired behavior:**
- **Charge the overlap at the maximum bitrate.** Each watch on the old connection is charged at its share's maximum bitrate:
  - from the tab's final report of the old connection, or from the watch's start if that is later;
  - until the instance closes that watch's pull on the old connection, or the tab is cut off, whichever comes first.

  It is counted twice when the old connection's last report said relayed, the same as the existing rule for a watch with no later report. A report from the new connection never cancels this charge.
- Decided in triage: the instance charges the overlap itself, by its existing rule for a watch with no later report. Making the tab report the old connection's total once more at retire was rejected. That would change the socket protocol, would mean keeping two running totals per tab, and would still lose the overlap whenever the socket dropped before the report. The maximum-bitrate charge reads a little high, which is the safe side for a budget.
- A replacement answered `unavailable` charges nothing extra. The tab keeps the old connection, its running total carries on, and its next report covers the time in between, as now.
- An abandoned replacement charges only the old connection's overlap, once. That includes one let go of because the tab asked again, or because the tab left.

**Key interfaces:**
- `Usage`: what it charges for a tab's watches between a final report and a new connection's reports. Today `newTotal` resets the running total, and a report deletes the tab's ended watches' gap rows. One of those has to stop removing the old connection's watches until they are charged.
- How a replacement tells usage that a watch on the old connection ended (the old pulls closed at the end of the replacement), and when.
- The `usage_tab` and `usage_watch` records. A schema change follows the repo's migration rules (CLAUDE.md, Migrations), including the test from earlier data.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- [ ] Seam 1 (usage): a tab watching one share sends its final report, then replaces its receiving connection, with the test's clock moved N seconds before `replaced`. The count includes N seconds of that share at its maximum bitrate, and the new connection's reports then charge from zero as now.
- [ ] The same, with the old connection last reported relayed, charges the overlap twice.
- [ ] A tab watching two shares is charged the overlap for each.
- [ ] A replacement answered `unavailable` charges no overlap, and the tab's next report charges only the difference from its last one.
- [ ] A replacement abandoned because the tab asked again charges the old connection's overlap once, not once for each attempt.
- [ ] Spec.md's section "Usage and the usage budget" and the header of the usage code state the rule.
- [ ] `pnpm typecheck`, `pnpm lint` and `pnpm test` are green.

**Out of scope:**
- The socket protocol and the tab's usage reports, which stay as they are.
- Reading Cloudflare's own usage figure.
- The safety margin and the rule that counts relayed watching twice.
- 62's hand-over and eviction cases, and 64's asking again.

### Done

**What changed.** Migration 0010 adds `usage_watch.old_connection`. `Usage.newTotal` sets it on each watch of the tab's that does not have it yet. It also pins the watch's `since` to the old connection's last report, or the watch's start if later, and its `relayed` to that report's. A watch that ended since that report is charged at once. From then on:
- a report deletes only `old_connection = 0` rows, so the new connection's totals never cancel the charge;
- `watchEnded` charges such a watch when its pull closes, even while the tab can report;
- `tabGone` ends any still open at the cut-off.

A second `newTotal`, from a replacement asked for again, leaves marked watches as they are, so the overlap is charged once. An `unavailable` replacement never calls `newTotal`. The header of `usage.ts` and the spec's "Gaps" bullet state the rule.

**Tests.**
- Seven new tests in `test/usage.test.ts` ("a replaced receiving connection"):
  - one share;
  - one share, relayed;
  - two shares;
  - a watch started after the final total;
  - `unavailable`;
  - asked again;
  - the tab leaving mid-replacement.
- One in `test/migrations.test.ts`: it starts on migrations 1 to 9 with a budget and a reset day, restarts on 10, and charges a replacement's overlap.
- Totals: 516 in the Workers runtime and 69 Playwright, with typecheck and lint.

**Review.**
- **Spec:** the reviewer suggested that a replacement asked again and then answered `unavailable` counts twice, since the old watches stay marked while the tab "reports on that connection". It does not report on it. The room client sends the abandoned replacement's total as its final one and goes on reporting that connection (`room-client.svelte.ts`, `replace`). So nothing ever reports the old connection again, and charging its watches until their pulls close is right. That the instance keeps the old connection while the tab holds the abandoned one is 64's to settle. `newTotal` also marks watches on a fresh `connect`. This is harmless, since a let-go receiving connection carries nothing, and keeps one rule.
- **Standards:**
  - Fixed: the stale schema-guard comment in `newTotal`, and an unused column read in `received`.
  - Left as they are:
    - the test helper `replaceWatching`, which differs from the two in `replacing.test.ts` and `connections.test.ts` in moving the clock;
    - the migration test adding no `usage_watch` rows before the restart. A report on the older schema already reads the new column, as 0009's test notes for its own.
